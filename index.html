Start with the question you need to answer

What needs to be documented, who will review it, and which systems are in scope? These questions shape the proposed inventory and the evidence it would need.

  1. Cryptographic bill of materials

    A proposed CycloneDX inventory of identified cryptographic assets and dependencies. A standard format makes a record easier to exchange; it does not establish that the inventory is complete.

  2. On-chain exposure records

    Observations about public-key exposure, signature-dependent contracts and administrative control paths, limited to the networks, source versions and blocks actually examined.

  3. Requirements and source mapping

    A record of the applicable question, the evidence that supports an answer and the parts that remain unverified or outside the assessment.

Every finding needs a source

The research focuses on reproducible evidence: a source location, a version or block reference, and the time of observation. Unknown values stay unknown.

Traceable observations

Source-code locations, contract addresses and transaction references can explain where a finding came from. Content hashes can help detect changes to a saved record.

Clear coverage gaps

Public repositories and blockchain data cannot by themselves establish an entire custody system's cryptography, private-key lifecycle or off-chain dependencies. A hash does not establish that a claim is true.

Why SignWarden

Evidence before certainty.

A decision about change starts with understanding what is already there.

Digital assets depend on cryptography across signatures, contracts and the systems around them. Before considering a post-quantum transition, teams need a clear view of those dependencies—and of the evidence they can actually inspect.

That is the question behind SignWarden: can a scoped, source-linked inventory make those dependencies and gaps easier to discuss? We are researching that question, with unknowns visible and claims limited to their evidence.

Defined scope. Clear limits.

Our current work evaluates whether this evidence package can answer a concrete due-diligence question. It does not certify quantum safety or regulatory compliance.

  • An exposed public key, a signature call and an exploitable asset-control path are different findings.
  • Supervisory requirements depend on the institution, its activities and the jurisdiction. A general inventory is not a legal assessment.
  • We are not offering migration consulting, a post-quantum wallet or a proprietary risk score.
  • We do not hold assets or request private keys, seed phrases or credentials.

Questions

Who is this work for?

We are exploring the needs of digital asset custody and security teams responding to a specific institutional review or supervisory question. The scope depends on the organisation and the question it needs to answer.

Can public blockchain data provide a complete inventory?

Public data can support some observations. It cannot by itself establish an entire custody system’s cryptography, internal controls or private-key lifecycle.

Does an inventory prove quantum safety or compliance?

No. An inventory records what is supported by the available evidence. It does not certify quantum safety, regulatory compliance or protection from an attack.

How does this relate to existing tools?

Existing scanners, blockchain interfaces and supplier records can provide useful inputs. Our current work evaluates the scope and evidence needed to connect those inputs to a specific review question.
