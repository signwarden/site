Research phase

Tracking attacks where AI agents meet crypto wallets

AI agents now operate wallets, sign transactions and call tools and packages on people's behalf, and attacks are following them there. SignWarden collects publicly reported attacks and malicious samples, links them to evidence and labels the techniques, so that affected people and researchers have a record they can check.

What SignWarden is

SignWarden is a threat-intelligence project focused on attacks at the intersection of AI agents and crypto wallets. It is in a research phase: we are building the data, the verification methods and the verdict process, and we do not offer a service yet.

What we do

  • Collect publicly reported attacks and malicious samples: packages, MCP tools, agent skills, browser and editor extensions, delegation contracts.
  • Link every verdict to evidence, keep what we saw at the time where we can (snapshots and hashes), and label the attack technique.
  • The most severe verdicts are confirmed by a human; an AI cannot decide them alone.

What we don't do

  • We do not intercept, sign or monitor your transactions. We are not a wallet or a browser extension.
  • We do not hold private keys, seed phrases or assets, and we will never ask you for them. Anyone claiming to be SignWarden and asking for them is scamming you.
  • We give no investment advice. We do not interact with malicious contracts and we do not run malicious samples.
  • There is currently no API, no data download and no paid plan.

What we track

  • Prompt injection: instructions planted in web pages, posts or documents that an agent reads.
  • Tool-description poisoning: instructions hidden in the description of an MCP tool.
  • Malicious or compromised packages, extensions and agent skills: impersonated names, or malicious versions published after an account takeover.
  • Exfiltration of private keys or seed phrases.
  • Malicious EIP-7702 delegations.
  • Tampering or credential theft by AI model relay (LLM API forwarding) services: under research; we do not claim to cover them and we do not claim to detect them.

Method and limits

  • We only collect public reports and research. We did not obtain or run the malicious code, and entries from third parties such as OSV have no independent second source.
  • Verdicts can be wrong. Anyone can appeal; when an appeal succeeds we add a correction record and keep the old one.
  • The data is not real-time and not guaranteed to be complete. Not being listed does not mean something is safe.
  • How the data will be published, and under what license, is still being planned; we will say so here once it is settled.

Responsible disclosure and contact

Email: security@signwarden.com

Write to us if:

  • you found a package, extension, agent skill or contract that you believe is malicious;
  • you believe a verdict is wrong and want to appeal or request a correction;
  • you found a security issue with this website.

Please do not include private keys, seed phrases or credentials. If what you found is an active theft, please also notify the affected registry or project maintainers. We will not disclose your identity without your consent. We are a small research project and will acknowledge reports as soon as we can, but we do not commit to a fixed response time. security.txt
